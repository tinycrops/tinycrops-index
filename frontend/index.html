tribev2-findings-pages

This page reports an in-silico neuroscience study built on Meta’s TRIBE v2 foundation model, treating the model as if it were an fMRI subject: controlled audio, text, image, and ASCII stimuli were fed in and the model’s predicted cortical surface responses were analyzed. The main findings are that zero-shot heard speech maps to canonical auditory cortex as expected, while an ASCII-cat LoRA finetune causes an ~8% shift in predicted brain maps that is not cat-specific and lands in language cortex rather than visual cortex; a larger “substantiality” test shows the effect generalizes to many hand-drawn animals, suggesting a style detector rather than a cat detector. This matters to neuroscientists and foundation-model researchers interested in how multimodal models encode stimuli, how to run brain-like localizer analyses without human data, and how to interpret apparent cross-modal effects versus generic distribution shifts.
