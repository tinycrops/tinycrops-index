This project built an in-silico neuroscience benchmark for TRIBE v2, Meta’s tri-modal model for predicting human fMRI responses, by feeding it controlled video, audio, text, and ASCII-art stimuli and measuring where its predicted cortical activation maps localized. The main findings were that zero-shot speech stimuli correctly localized to auditory cortex, while an ASCII-cat LoRA caused an ~8% shift that was not cat-specific and did not move activity into visual cortex; when scaled across 86 hand-drawn animals, the effect was substantial but generic, pointing to a line-art/style detector rather than a cat detector. These results matter to researchers studying multimodal foundation models, interpretability, and brain-model alignment, because they show both what TRIBE v2 captures reliably and where apparent cross-modal effects disappear under stronger controls.
